# Brightside Health — Account Research

**Territory:** Behavioral Health
**Use case:** Customer acquisition
**Verified contacts:** Brad Kittredge, CEO & Co-Founder; Chris Pastorius, Head of Growth
**Research date:** 2026-07-29

## What Brightside Health does

Brightside Health is a virtual mental health company delivering psychiatry (medication management) and therapy for depression, anxiety, and other mood disorders, including patients with elevated suicide risk. Its model pairs a proprietary clinical decision-support platform ("PrecisionRx") with a licensed clinician network to match patients to treatment quickly, with appointments available in as little as 48 hours.

## Scale and market position

- Founded in 2017; positions itself as a more clinically rigorous alternative to consumer telehealth platforms, with leadership including a former Stanford psychiatry chief.
- In-network coverage now spans **100M+ covered lives** through payers including Cigna/Evernorth, Aetna, UnitedHealthcare/Optum, and regional Blues plans, plus expanding Medicare and Medicaid partnerships (Centene, Optum, Lucet, BCBS Texas).
- First telepsychiatry company to reach 100% national coverage for Medicare Part B beneficiaries.
- Offers a full acuity spectrum: standard medication/therapy plans, a Crisis Care program for active suicidality, and virtual intensive outpatient programs (including for substance use, via its Lionrock Recovery acquisition).
- Company size roughly 51–200 employees per public org data.
- Self-pay pricing is tiered ($95–$349/month depending on plan), with insurance coverage varying by state.

## Customer experience today

### Consumer journey

1. A visitor chooses a track: medication management, therapy, or combined therapy + medication (plus specialized Crisis Care or IOP tracks in select states).
2. Insurance is checked or self-pay pricing is shown; a care navigator can verify coverage for higher-acuity programs.
3. Patients are matched with a clinician and can typically book within 48 hours.
4. Ongoing care includes weekly check-ins, asynchronous messaging with providers, and data-driven treatment adjustment.

### Observed acquisition strengths

- Strong clinical-credibility positioning (published outcomes research, PrecisionRx model, Stanford-affiliated leadership).
- Broad and growing insurance/payer footprint, including underserved Medicare/Medicaid populations.
- Differentiated track for high-acuity patients (Crisis Care, IOP) that competitors often avoid.

### Where a conversational layer may help

This is an inference from public information, not an assertion of a problem:

- A visitor’s situation often spans multiple tracks at once — e.g., "I'm on Medicaid, need medication and therapy, and have had suicidal thoughts" — which may not map cleanly to a single self-serve plan selector.
- Insurance/coverage verification and program eligibility (standard vs. Crisis Care vs. IOP) are exactly the kind of practical, non-clinical questions a governed conversational agent can pre-qualify before a live navigator call.
- Given the sensitivity of the population served (elevated suicide risk), the moment before a human conversation is a high-value point to capture context accurately and escalate appropriately.

## Why Brightside Health should care about Tars

Tars can support a **strictly non-clinical, governed** first conversation:

- Answer approved questions on plans (medication/therapy/combined), insurance/self-pay, program eligibility, and what to expect at intake.
- Collect state, new-vs-returning status, insurance/self-pay status, and program interest (standard vs. Crisis Care vs. IOP) before handoff.
- Route visitors into Brightside's existing plan-selection, insurance-verification, or care-navigator workflow.
- Pass a structured interaction summary to the care navigator team so patients don't repeat themselves.
- Escalate immediately to defined human and crisis resources whenever risk indicators appear — this is not a decision the agent should make.

## Contact-specific angles

### Brad Kittredge — CEO & Co-Founder

Brad's public commentary consistently centers on one problem: people trying to use insurance for mental health face long waits and directories full of clinicians who don't call back. The pitch is a front-door problem — Brightside already solves the supply/access side (48-hour appointments, broad payer network); Tars can make sure no high-intent, insurance-eligible visitor is lost before reaching that access.

### Chris Pastorius — Head of Growth

Chris owns digital performance marketing and growth for Brightside. The pitch is a conversion-efficiency problem: Brightside is actively expanding into new payer segments (Medicare, Medicaid) and acuity tiers (Crisis Care, IOP); a conversational qualification layer can make sure paid and organic traffic converts into correctly-routed, insurance-verified leads rather than generic form fills.
