# Rula Health — Account Research

**Territory:** Behavioral Health
**Use case:** Customer acquisition
**Verified contacts:** Andrew Mok, Chief Marketing Officer; Laura Palmer, Chief Revenue Officer
**Research date:** 2026-07-29

## What Rula Health does

Rula (formerly Path Mental Health) is a national behavioral health provider group delivering insurance-covered therapy and psychiatry, virtually and in some markets in-person, for individuals age 5+, couples, and families.

## Scale and market position

- **21,000+ providers** across all 50 states and D.C. (up from ~9,000–10,000 in 2024/early 2025 — rapid network growth).
- Psychiatric services now offered in most states; therapy for ages 5+, couples, and families.
- **120M+ covered lives** through Aetna/CVS, Anthem/Elevance, Cigna/Evernorth, Kaiser, UnitedHealthcare/Optum, and many Blue Cross Blue Shield plans, plus Medicare and Medicaid in select states.
- Typical cost with insurance is around $15/session; self-pay transparent pricing ($150–$225).
- Public promise: seen in as little as 48 hours from signup.

## Customer experience today

### Consumer journey

1. Visitor enters basic details and insurance information.
2. Rula's system matches them to an in-network provider, aiming for availability within ~48 hours.
3. Booking happens through a web portal; sessions run over Zoom.
4. Rula positions itself as "a national front door to in-network, comprehensive behavioral health services."

### Observed acquisition strengths

- One of the largest all-50-states, insurance-covered networks in the category, which simplifies the core "will they take my insurance" question for most visitors.
- Strong, fast time-to-first-appointment promise (48 hours) as a headline acquisition claim.
- Recently strengthened leadership specifically to professionalize growth: Laura Palmer (ex-Google Cloud, ex-Unity Technologies VP of Revenue, scaled Unity's GTM past $1B pre-IPO) as CRO, and Andrew Mok (12 years at Turo, scaled it into a multi-billion-dollar global marketplace) as CMO — both joined September 2025.

### Where a conversational layer may help

This is an inference from public information, not an assertion of a problem:

- A visitor matching request depends on accurately capturing insurance plan, age/relationship type (individual, couple, child), and psychiatry-vs-therapy need before matching — several compound inputs that a simple form may handle less gracefully than a guided conversation.
- With a network that recently roughly doubled in size, matching quality depends heavily on the completeness of what's captured at intake; a conversational front end can probe for missing detail (e.g., specific plan variant, preferred modality) before handoff.
- The "48-hour" promise is a strong claim to market against — the more precisely intake captures true availability needs upfront, the more consistently that promise can be kept without needing a second contact.

## Why Rula should care about Tars

Tars can support a **strictly non-clinical, governed** first conversation:

- Answer approved questions about coverage, the matching process, and what "seen within 48 hours" means in practice.
- Collect insurance plan, relationship/care type (individual, couple, child/family), therapy-vs-psychiatry interest, and format preference.
- Hand off a clean, structured summary into Rula's existing matching system.
- Escalate immediately to human support or crisis resources where appropriate.
