## What Acadia does

Acadia Healthcare is the largest stand-alone behavioral-health company in the United States. It operates a network of behavioral-health facilities serving children, adolescents, adults, and seniors across inpatient psychiatric care, specialty treatment, residential treatment, outpatient care, detox, partial hospitalization (PHP), intensive outpatient (IOP), and medication-assisted treatment.

## Scale and market position

- Acadia’s current executive page reports **250 facilities in 39 states and Puerto Rico**, approximately **23,000 employees**, and **75,000+ patients served daily** across four service lines.
- Its public “Why Choose Us” page separately cites 227 locations, 9,900+ inpatient/residential beds, and coverage in 40 states/Puerto Rico; the mismatch reflects different publication dates. Use the executive-page figure as the current company summary.
- Acadia provides national services including a behavioral-health helpline and treatment-placement specialists.

## Customer experience today

### Observed consumer journey

1. A person or family can explore disorders, populations, programs, levels of care, and a facility-location network on Acadia’s corporate site.
2. They can call a behavioral-health helpline available 24/7, use the treatment-facility finder, or reach national placement services.
3. The appropriate local facility/admissions team handles insurance verification, clinical assessment, eligibility, and treatment placement.
4. Acadia’s site clearly directs people in life-threatening emergencies to emergency services.

### Strengths and opportunity

- Acadia has unmatched breadth of settings, populations, and locations, plus national access services.
- The same breadth can make the first digital decision hard: a visitor may know they need help but not know the appropriate care setting, location, program, insurance path, or contact route.
- A governed conversational layer can answer only approved logistical questions, collect simple routing context, and direct the visitor to Acadia’s existing helpline, finder, placement specialist, or local admissions path. This is an inference from public pages, not an assertion of a current operational problem.

## Why Acadia should care about Tars

Tars can support a **strictly non-clinical, governed** access experience at network scale:

- Answer approved questions on locations, populations served, program types, insurance process, and how to begin.
- Capture minimal context such as ZIP/state, adult/child/adolescent/senior pathway, general concern category, insurance/self-pay, and preferred contact method.
- Route to existing facility finder, helpline, placement specialist, or appropriate human admissions process with a concise handoff summary.
- Enforce emergency and crisis escalation; do not diagnose, determine level of care, assess eligibility, recommend treatment, or replace clinical/admissions judgment.

The test is whether more high-intent visitors arrive at the correct existing human access route with less confusion and less repeated discovery.
