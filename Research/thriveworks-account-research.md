# Thriveworks — Account Research

**Territory:** Behavioral Health  
**Use case:** Customer acquisition  
**Verified contacts:** Kabir Daya, Chief Digital Officer & Chief Marketing Officer; Michael Midgette, Chief Growth Officer  
**Research date:** 2026-07-29

## What Thriveworks does

Thriveworks provides outpatient therapy and psychiatry through a national mix of local offices and virtual care. Its consumer offering spans individual, couples, family, child/teen therapy and psychiatry/medication management.

## Scale and market position

- **2,600+ licensed clinicians** and **340+ U.S. locations**.
- **1.9M+ sessions** completed in the last year.
- **360+ insurance plans** accepted according to the homepage; the provider-search experience currently references 585+ plans.
- Thriveworks positions itself around quick access: appointments can be available as soon as the same week, with online, evening and weekend options.

## Customer experience today

### Consumer journey

1. A visitor selects a care type, location and insurance/self-pay option.
2. They browse available providers, including specialties, offerings and availability.
3. They can book online or call a scheduling line.
4. If no opening is currently available, Thriveworks collects contact details and offers to notify the visitor when a match opens.
5. Scheduling support is available seven days per week; the contact page differentiates booking, billing, insurance verification and clinical-quality support.

### Observed acquisition strengths

- Clear coverage and availability positioning.
- Multiple conversion routes: provider search, online booking, callback/waitlist capture and phone scheduling.
- Strong flexibility story: virtual/in-person, evenings/weekends and broad insurance participation.

### Where a conversational layer may help

This is an inference from public pages, not an assertion of a problem:

- A new visitor may have a compound question that does not map cleanly to a single filter: “I need a child therapist, have this plan, need evenings, and prefer virtual.”
- The journey distinguishes care type, geography, insurance and availability; a conversational step can connect those inputs before a visitor reaches a no-opening screen or phone queue.
- If availability is constrained in a local market, the moment of no match is a high-value opportunity to preserve intent and offer an appropriate human follow-up.

## Why Thriveworks should care about Tars

Tars can support a **strictly non-clinical, governed** first conversation:

- Answer approved questions on service type, virtual/in-person options, availability process, insurance and booking.
- Collect state/ZIP, new-vs-returning status, service interest, insurance/self-pay, format and time preferences.
- Guide visitors into Thriveworks’ existing provider search, booking, callback or scheduling-support paths.
- Pass a concise interaction summary so staff do not ask the visitor to repeat basic context.
- Escalate immediately to defined human and crisis resources where the agent is not the right channel.
