# Brightline — Account Research

**Territory:** Behavioral Health
**Use case:** Customer acquisition
**Verified contacts:** Elizabeth Hart, Chief Marketing Officer; Kelsey Mellard, Chief Growth Officer
**Research date:** 2026-07-29

## What Brightline does

Brightline is a family mental health company supporting kids, teens (ages 0–17), and their parents/caregivers with therapy, coaching, and psychiatric services, delivered virtually and — as of a 2025 leadership-driven expansion — through a growing hybrid, in-person model.

## Scale and market position

- Recognized on Fast Company's World's 50 Most Innovative Companies list and as a Fierce 15 honoree.
- ~190 employees; approximately $35M annual revenue as of mid-2025.
- Runs BrightLife Kids, a free digital behavioral-health-coaching program for families in California.
- Actively expanding from a virtual-only model into hybrid care, including a flagship in-person clinic expansion into Brooklyn, and deepening insurer partnerships to expand in-network coverage.
- Strategic partnership with Teladoc Health for virtual mental healthcare delivery to children and families.

## Customer experience today

### Consumer journey

- Parents/caregivers are the primary acquisition audience, even though the child or teen is the patient — a two-audience dynamic (the person searching vs. the person receiving care) that's more layered than most consumer behavioral health categories.
- Brightline is mid-transition from virtual-only to hybrid, meaning the "is there an in-person option near me" question is newly relevant and not yet uniformly answerable everywhere.
- The company is actively working with insurers to expand in-network coverage, meaning coverage answers may vary meaningfully by plan and by region during this transition.

### Observed acquisition strengths

- Strong innovation/trust signals (Fast Company, Fierce 15) useful for a category where parents are making a high-trust decision on behalf of a child.
- A free program (BrightLife Kids) that can serve as a lower-commitment entry point in at least one state.
- Leadership recently and specifically strengthened for growth: Kelsey Mellard (Chief Growth Officer, ex-founder of Sitka, HHS innovation background) and Elizabeth Hart (CMO, ex-Chief Customer & Engagement Officer at Eleanor Health, with acquisition/retention experience at Cigna and AOL) both joined in March 2025 specifically to drive this hybrid expansion.

### Where a conversational layer may help

This is an inference from public information, not an assertion of a problem:

- A parent's question is rarely simple: it usually combines the child's age, a specific concern, insurance plan, and now — during the hybrid transition — a location/format question that may not have a consistent answer yet.
- Because Brightline is actively adding in-person options market by market, a static page listing coverage risks going stale faster than a conversational flow that can be updated centrally and can be honest about "not yet available here, but here's what is."
- The parent (searcher) and child (patient) being different people adds a layer most intake forms aren't built to handle gracefully — e.g., distinguishing "who is this appointment for" early and clearly.

## Why Brightline should care about Tars

Tars can support a **strictly non-clinical, governed** first conversation:

- Answer approved questions about virtual vs. in-person availability by region, insurance participation, and what to expect from a first appointment.
- Collect child's age range, presenting concern category, insurance/self-pay status, and format preference (virtual/in-person, if available locally).
- Route to Brightline's existing booking or waitlist path, and clearly flag regions where in-person isn't yet available rather than leaving that ambiguous.
- Escalate immediately to human support or crisis resources — especially important given the pediatric context.

## Contact-specific angles

Hart's prior role focused specifically on customer acquisition and retention at Cigna, Everside Health, and AOL, and she most recently led customer/engagement experience at Eleanor Health. The pitch is a familiar one for her: converting more of Brightline's paid and organic demand into completed bookings during a period when the underlying service footprint (virtual vs. hybrid) is actively changing underneath the marketing.

### Kelsey Mellard — Chief Growth Officer

Mellard's background spans HHS policy/technology innovation and founding Sitka, a value-based-care network — someone used to thinking about access and growth as the same problem. The pitch is an access-and-growth framing: as Brightline expands market by market into hybrid care, a conversational front door can keep the "what's available to my family right now" answer accurate in real time, rather than lagging the expansion.
