# Grow Therapy — Account Research

**Territory:** Behavioral Health
**Use case:** Customer acquisition
**Verified contacts:** Marshall Ball, SVP, Marketing; Jason Oringher, Director, Performance Marketing
**Research date:** 2026-07-29

## What Grow Therapy does

Grow Therapy is a managed marketplace connecting clients with independent, licensed therapists and psychiatric providers who accept insurance. Unlike employed-clinician models, Grow's providers are independent contractors who use Grow's platform for credentialing, billing, and client acquisition.

## Scale and market position

- **25,000+ psychiatric and mental-health providers** nationwide; 90+ specialties covered.
- **230M+ people** covered through 100+ insurance plans, including Aetna, Cigna, UnitedHealthcare, Medicaid, and Medicare (availability varies by state).
- 90% of clients report finding a provider that fits their needs; most clients start within 2 days.
- Just raised a **$150M Series D** (announced June 2026), and is actively extending its platform to employers — specifically targeting the gap where someone exhausts an Employee Assistance Program (EAP) benefit and previously had to switch therapists or pay out-of-pocket to continue with insurance.
- Average client cost with insurance is ~$21/session; self-pay range $75–$250+, with sliding-scale options.

## Customer experience today

### Consumer journey

1. A prospective client shares goals, preferences, and insurance information.
2. They search and filter more than 25,000 providers by specialty, language, identity, availability, and treatment approach.
3. Booking happens directly in the therapist's calendar, typically within about 2 days.
4. Grow's own data shows 65% of clients paid less than they expected.

### Observed acquisition strengths

- Extremely broad specialty filtering (90+ specialties) supports highly specific client needs.
- Transparent, favorable cost framing (average $21/session, most under-expectation).
- New employer-channel expansion directly solves a real, named pain point (EAP-to-insurance transition) rather than a generic access problem.
- Recently added senior marketing leadership specifically to build brand at scale: Marshall Ball (ex-SVP Brand Marketing at The Farmer's Dog; earlier brand work at Expedia, Apple Services, Wieden+Kennedy, 72andSunny for ESPN, Nike, Delta) joined October 2025 reporting to CEO Jake Cooper.

### Where a conversational layer may help

This is an inference from public information, not an assertion of a problem:

- A self-directed filter across 90+ specialties, plus language, identity, and availability, is powerful but assumes the visitor already knows how to translate their situation into the right filter combination — the same "compound question before the first filter" pattern seen across this category.
- The new employer/EAP-transition use case is inherently a multi-step, slightly complex situation (a benefit is running out, insurance needs to take over, ideally with the same therapist) that a static form is unlikely to handle gracefully on its own.
- With 25,000+ providers, ensuring a first search returns genuinely well-matched results depends on the specificity of what's captured upfront — a conversational front end can gather that detail incrementally rather than requiring the visitor to know which filters to set.

## Why Grow Therapy should care about Tars

Tars can support a **strictly non-clinical, governed** first conversation:

- Answer approved questions about how the marketplace, insurance credentialing, and matching process work.
- Collect service/specialty interest, insurance/self-pay status, language and identity preferences (where volunteered), and — notably — EAP-to-insurance transition status for the new employer channel.
- Route into Grow's existing search/filter and booking experience with a pre-populated, more precise starting point.
- Escalate immediately to human support or crisis resources where appropriate.
