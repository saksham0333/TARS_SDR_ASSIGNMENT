# Charlie Health — Account Research

**Territory:** Behavioral Health
**Use case:** Customer acquisition
**Verified contacts:** Carter Barnhart, Co-founder & CEO; Noah Weissmann, VP, Growth Operations
**Research date:** 2026-07-29

## What Charlie Health does

Charlie Health is a virtual intensive outpatient program (IOP) provider for high-acuity mental health and substance use conditions, serving clients ages 8–64. Its model combines multiple weekly group sessions, individual therapy, family therapy and psychiatric support into a structured 9–12 week virtual program, positioned between traditional once-weekly therapy and inpatient/residential care.

## Scale and market position

- Founded in 2020 by Carter Barnhart and Dr. Caroline Fenkel; described as the largest virtual provider of high-acuity behavioral health treatment for youth in crisis.
- Operates in roughly 39–40 states, having grown from its original Montana launch.
- Covered by **600+ insurance plans**, including Cigna, Blue Cross Blue Shield, Aetna, Anthem, Humana, TRICARE, UnitedHealthcare and Medicaid in states where IOP is covered.
- Reports strong outcomes data: clients maintain meaningful reductions in depression and anxiety symptoms a year after treatment, and roughly 95% avoid readmission within 6 months.
- Most clients begin treatment within 24 hours of completing intake, with insurance verified on the first call.
- Includes 24/7 crisis care, 20+ free family support groups, peer mentors and an alumni program as part of the standard offering.

## Customer experience today

### Consumer journey

1. A prospective client or family reaches out (often via referral, provider, or web) and completes a clinical intake assessment.
2. The Admissions Team verifies insurance on the first call; the Benefits Team walks through any potential costs.
3. Clients are matched to a personalized treatment plan (group cadence, individual therapy, family therapy, medication-assisted treatment if needed) and typically start within 24 hours.
4. Ongoing programming includes group and individual sessions plus access to 24/7 crisis support and alumni community programming after discharge.

### Observed acquisition strengths

- Fast intake-to-start time (24 hours) once insurance is verified.
- Broad insurance acceptance, including Medicaid in many states — reduces a major access barrier for high-acuity care.
- Strong, published outcomes data used as a credibility anchor in marketing and referral conversations.
- Differentiated position: fills the "missing middle" between weekly therapy and inpatient care.

### Where a conversational layer may help

This is an inference from public information, not an assertion of a problem:

- A prospective client or worried family member reaching out is often in crisis or near-crisis and may not know whether IOP, rather than standard therapy or inpatient care, is the right fit — a compound question a self-serve form doesn't resolve.
- Insurance/Medicaid eligibility varies by state and plan; pre-qualifying this in conversation, before the Admissions Team call, could reduce friction at exactly the moment urgency is highest.
- Because many inquiries come through referral partners (providers, schools, discharge planners) as well as direct-to-consumer channels, a conversational layer could help route each type of inquiry appropriately and pass context along instead of restarting intake from zero.

## Why Charlie Health should care about Tars

Tars can support a **strictly non-clinical, governed** first conversation:

- Answer approved questions on what IOP is, how it differs from weekly therapy or inpatient care, insurance/Medicaid coverage, and what intake involves.
- Collect state, new-vs-returning status, referral source, insurance/self-pay status, and urgency level before handoff.
- Route inquiries into Charlie Health's existing intake, Admissions Team, or insurance-verification workflow.
- Pass a structured interaction summary so Admissions doesn't ask a distressed family to repeat themselves.
- Escalate immediately to defined human and crisis resources whenever risk indicators appear — never a decision left to the agent, especially given the high-acuity, often crisis-adjacent population Charlie Health serves.

## Contact-specific angles

### Carter Barnhart — Co-founder & CEO

Carter founded Charlie Health from personal experience with the gaps in accessible, high-quality behavioral healthcare — long waitlists, capacity constraints, and insurance barriers. The pitch is a mission-alignment problem: as demand scales past 39+ states and 600+ payers, a governed conversational layer helps make sure no family in crisis gets lost in the first few minutes of reaching out, echoing exactly the access gap she's spoken about publicly.

### Noah Weissmann — VP, Growth Operations

Noah owns growth strategy and operations at Charlie Health, spanning both supply and demand sides of the business. The pitch is an operational-efficiency and conversion problem: pre-qualifying inquiries (state, insurance, urgency, referral source) before they reach Admissions can improve capacity planning and reduce time spent by the growth/ops team on unqualified or misrouted leads.
