# Pneuma Behavioral Health — Cold-Call Scripts

## Tammy George — Chief Growth Officer

### Opening

> Hi Tammy, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know this is unexpected—can I take 30 seconds to explain why I called, and you can decide whether it is relevant?

### Reason for the call

> I was reviewing Pneuma’s partnership journey for clinician-owned practices. The value proposition is compelling—more operating and growth support without losing identity—but a prospective owner may have several questions before they will submit a general inquiry: size, state, service lines, billing, staffing, expansion, and independence. That first digital moment is why I called.

### Value proposition

> Tars gives growth teams a governed conversational acquisition layer. It answers only Pneuma-approved questions, captures practice profile, operating challenge, growth objective and timing, then routes a structured summary into the existing partnership workflow. It does not make financial, legal, clinical, or transaction commitments.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. Where do prospective partners most often stall before booking a first conversation?
2. Which questions do your business-development team repeatedly answer on the initial call?
3. How do you prioritize a solo practice versus a multi-site group from a web inquiry today?
4. Is website-to-qualified-partnership conversion a current growth priority?

### Close

> If that is material, could we schedule 20 minutes with growth and business development? We can map one narrow partner journey and decide whether a controlled pilot has a measurable case.

### Objection handling

**“Partnership evaluation needs a human conversation.”**

> Completely agree. Tars is designed to make that human conversation better prepared, not replace it. It handles the approved introductory questions and gives your team context.

**“Our partnerships are too nuanced for automation.”**

> The transaction is nuanced; the first routing conversation can be narrow. We would use your exact approved language and only qualify context your team already needs.

## Albert Lupin — Chief Executive Officer & Founder

### Opening

> Hi Albert, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know I caught you out of the blue—may I have 30 seconds to explain why I reached out, and you can decide whether it is relevant?

### Reason for the call

> Pneuma’s model is built around helping clinician-owned organizations scale while preserving what makes them trusted locally. I wondered how a practice leader who is curious—but uncertain about fit—gets from the first website visit to the right human conversation without having to interpret a broad set of services alone.

### Value proposition

> Tars can provide a consistent, governed partnership front door using Pneuma-approved content. It can clarify the support model, gather basic practice and goal context, and hand off to the current team with a summary. Your team remains in control of all partnership evaluation, commitments, and next steps.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. Is simplifying the initial partnership inquiry part of Pneuma’s expansion agenda?
2. What information would make the first business-development call more valuable for both sides?
3. Where do practice owners most often need reassurance about independence and identity?
4. Which service or market would make a clean pilot meaningful?

### Close

> If one pathway is worth improving, would it be unreasonable to schedule 20 minutes with the partnership and operating owners? We can assess a single use case without proposing a broad platform change.

### Objection handling

**“We want the first interaction to feel personal.”**

> That is the aim. The agent can offer an immediate, tailored approved answer and then hand the person to a human with context, rather than make them wait after a blank form.

**“We are focused on operations, not more technology.”**

> The test would be operational: does it give the existing growth team better-qualified inquiries and reduce repetitive first-touch work? If not, it is not worth expanding.
