# Sero Mental Health — Cold-Call Scripts

## Daniel Krasner — Chief Growth Officer

### Opening

> Hi Daniel, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know this is unexpected—can I take 30 seconds to explain why I called, and you can decide if it is relevant?

### Reason for the call

> I saw Sero’s focus on access, growth, and ethical patient acquisition. Your network has specialized centers and direct admissions paths, but a prospective patient or family member may not know which center or program fits before they start navigating sites and phone numbers. That is the first-touch friction I’m calling about.

### Value proposition

> Tars provides a governed, non-clinical AI conversation using Sero-approved content. It can answer operational questions, collect minimal routing details, and hand the visitor to the correct existing center or admissions team with their context intact. It does not diagnose, recommend care, or automate clinical decisions.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. Where do visitors most often drop before reaching an admissions conversation?
2. How do you currently help someone who is unsure which Sero center or program to contact?
3. Does admissions receive enough digital context to avoid repeating basic discovery?
4. Is there a growth priority where a more qualified first handoff would be measurable?

### Close

> If that is material, could we schedule 20 minutes with growth and admissions leadership? We can map one narrow path and decide whether a controlled pilot has a measurable case.

### Objection handling

**“We already have phone numbers and center websites.”**

> Those remain the destination. Tars helps the visitor who is not yet sure which existing destination is appropriate get there with less friction.

**“We cannot have AI providing mental-health advice.”**

> Agreed. The scope is deliberately non-clinical—approved program, location, insurance and process information—with clear human and crisis escalation rules.

## Kristen Scheel — Chief Executive Officer

### Opening

> Hi Kristen, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know this is an unexpected call—may I take 30 seconds to explain why I reached out, and you can tell me if it is relevant?

### Reason for the call

> I was looking at Sero’s nationwide network of specialized behavioral-health centers. The portfolio offers real breadth, yet that can make the first online step difficult for someone who knows they need help but does not know which program or center should receive the inquiry. Given your responsibility for the network, I thought it might be relevant.

### Value proposition

> Tars can create a consistent, governed first conversation across the network. It handles only approved operational information, captures basic routing context, and transfers that context to the current center/admissions process. Sero and each center retain control over content, routing, human handoff, and clinical boundaries.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. How consistent is the initial digital inquiry experience across Sero’s center brands today?
2. Where do visitors have to repeat information after an online inquiry or contact-form submission?
3. Which guardrails would be non-negotiable for a network-level conversational experience?
4. Is simplifying the path to the right admissions team an access or operating priority this year?

### Close

> If one pathway is worth improving, would it be unreasonable to schedule 20 minutes with the operating and admissions owners? We can map a single use case without proposing a broad platform change.

### Objection handling

**“Each program is different.”**

> Exactly. The model is shared governance, not one generic script: a common safety and reporting framework, with center-specific content, routing and escalation rules.

**“This sounds like an admissions project.”**

> Admissions should own the handoff design. It is also an enterprise experience issue because the interaction begins before a person knows which center to contact.
