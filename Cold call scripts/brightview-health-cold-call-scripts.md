# BrightView Health — Cold-Call Scripts

## Jennifer Dumiak — Vice President of Growth

### Opening

> Hi Jen, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know this is unexpected—can I take 30 seconds to explain why I called, and you can decide whether it is relevant?

### Reason for the call

> I was reviewing BrightView’s public access journey: people can call, schedule online, find a location, or walk in, with an emphasis on beginning care quickly. The friction I’m curious about is the person who is ready to seek help but has a practical question about coverage, timing, location, or first-day expectations before they take that next step.

### Value proposition

> Tars gives behavioral-health teams a governed, non-clinical AI conversation. It answers BrightView-approved operational questions, gathers minimal routing context, and sends a visitor into the existing scheduling, call, or center workflow with that context preserved. It does not provide diagnosis, medication advice, or treatment eligibility decisions.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. Where do high-intent visitors tend to pause before scheduling or calling?
2. Which process questions create the most inbound admissions volume?
3. Does your team know why a visitor abandons after reaching a location or insurance page?
4. Which page or market would be a meaningful, measurable access pilot?

### Close

> If that is a real issue, could we schedule 20 minutes with growth and admissions? We can map one narrow journey and decide whether a controlled test has a measurable case.

### Objection handling

**“We already have online scheduling.”**

> That remains the destination. Tars helps a visitor who is not yet ready to select a slot get the approved information and choose the right existing route.

**“We cannot let AI advise patients.”**

> Agreed. The scope is specifically non-clinical: approved location, insurance, process, and scheduling information, with defined human and emergency escalation.

## Chad Smith — Chief Executive Officer

### Opening

> Hi Chad, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know I caught you out of the blue—may I have 30 seconds to explain why I called, and you can tell me whether it is relevant?

### Reason for the call

> BrightView has built an access-focused outpatient model across five states: call, online scheduling, weekday walk-ins, and an aim to begin care quickly. At that scale, I wondered how consistently a visitor with an unanswered operational question gets to the appropriate existing local process without losing momentum.

### Value proposition

> Tars is a governed, non-clinical conversational layer that can operate across BrightView’s digital entry points. It uses only approved content, captures minimal routing context, and gives the existing human/team workflow a structured handoff. BrightView retains clinical assessment, safety, and local routing control.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. How consistent is the first digital inquiry experience across locations today?
2. Where do visitors have to repeat information after they call or schedule?
3. Which safety and compliance requirements would be essential for a conversational experience?
4. Is improving pre-admissions access a current enterprise priority?

### Close

> If this is worth improving, would it be unreasonable to schedule 20 minutes with the access and operations owners? We can assess one path without proposing a broad platform change.

### Objection handling

**“Each location runs differently.”**

> That is why the model uses shared enterprise guardrails with location-specific routing, hours, and human-handoff rules.

**“This is an admissions issue.”**

> Admissions should co-own the design. It is also an enterprise access issue because the experience begins before a patient reaches a local team.
