# Brightside Health — Cold-Call Scripts

## Brad Kittredge — CEO & Co-Founder

### Opening

> Hi Brad, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know this is an unexpected call—can I take 30 seconds to explain why I called, and you can decide whether it's relevant?

### Reason for the call

> I've been following Brightside's growth into Medicare, Medicaid and higher-acuity care like Crisis Care and IOP. You've talked publicly about how hard it is for people to use insurance for mental health and actually reach someone. As Brightside adds more payers and more programs, the first-conversation problem gets harder too—matching a visitor's specific situation to the right track before they get lost. Given that you're the founder and the one setting that access mission, I thought it might be relevant.

### Value proposition

> Tars gives behavioral-health teams a HIPAA-ready agent for the non-clinical first conversation. It uses Brightside-approved content to answer questions on plans, insurance and program eligibility, gathers the right routing details, and hands the visitor to your existing plan-selection or care-navigator workflow. It never makes a clinical or risk decision—that stays fully with your team and escalation protocols.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. As you add new payer partnerships, how do visitors currently find out if their specific plan is accepted before talking to a navigator?
2. How does someone who might need Crisis Care or IOP get identified and routed today versus someone who just needs standard therapy?
3. Is reducing friction between a paid or organic visit and a verified, bookable lead a current growth priority?
4. Does your care-navigator team get enough context up front, or do they re-ask basic questions on the first call?

### Close

> If any of that is a real gap, could we schedule 20 minutes with you and whoever owns patient access or growth ops? We'd map one narrow journey—say, insurance verification or program routing—and see if a pilot has a measurable case.

### Objection handling

**"We already verify insurance and route patients through our navigator team."**

> That's the destination, and it should stay exactly as it is. Tars sits before that call—making sure the navigator gets a visitor who's already been asked the right questions and is a legitimate fit.

**"We can't have AI making clinical or risk judgments."**

> Agreed, and it wouldn't. The agent's scope is deliberately non-clinical: plan options, insurance, and process. Any indication of risk triggers immediate escalation to your team and crisis resources—never a bot decision.

## Chris Pastorius — Head of Growth

### Opening

> Hi Chris, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know I caught you out of the blue—may I have 30 seconds to explain why I called, and you can tell me whether it's relevant?

### Reason for the call

> I was looking at how Brightside has scaled its payer footprint to 100 million-plus covered lives, plus newer Medicare, Medicaid and Crisis Care programs. That's a lot of distinct paths for a visitor to self-select into. The growth question is whether every high-intent visitor—organic or paid—reaches the right plan and gets verified without dropping off first.

### Value proposition

> Tars can run a governed, non-clinical first conversation using Brightside-approved information. It captures insurance status, plan interest and program fit, then routes the visitor into your existing plan-selection or navigator workflow with a summary attached. The goal isn't a chatbot metric—it's fewer lost high-intent visits and a better-qualified handoff to your team.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. Where do you see the biggest drop-off between an ad click or landing page visit and a completed intake?
2. How do you currently handle visitors who aren't sure if their insurance is accepted or which program fits them?
3. Does your team have visibility into what visitors were actually looking for when they don't convert?
4. Would a pilot on one landing page—say Medicare or Crisis Care—be a clean way to test this without touching your core funnel?

### Close

> If one of those is material, would it be unreasonable to set up 20 minutes with you and whoever owns patient access? We can build a simple business case around one journey rather than propose a broad platform change.

### Objection handling

**"We already run paid campaigns with dedicated landing pages."**

> That's exactly where this fits best—Tars would sit on that landing page to qualify and route the visitor immediately, rather than sending everyone to the same static form.

**"We're cautious about anything touching insurance or clinical eligibility."**

> Understood—the agent doesn't make eligibility determinations. It collects what the visitor believes about their coverage and situation, and your navigator team still does the actual verification and clinical intake.
