# Thriveworks — Cold-Call Scripts

## Kabir Daya — Chief Digital Officer & Chief Marketing Officer

### Opening

> Hi Kabir, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know this is an unexpected call—can I take 30 seconds to explain why I called, and you can decide whether it is relevant?

### Reason for the call

> I was reviewing Thriveworks’ public acquisition experience. A client can select care type, location and insurance, search providers, book online or call support. That is a strong path. The harder case is the person with a combined question—say, a service need, an insurance plan and an evening virtual preference—before they know which filter or page to use. Given that you lead digital, product and marketing, I thought it might be relevant.

### Value proposition

> Tars gives behavioral-health teams a HIPAA-ready agent for the non-clinical first conversation. It uses approved Thriveworks content to answer practical questions, gathers a few routing details and moves the visitor into the existing provider-search, book-online, callback or scheduling-support workflow. It does not make clinical decisions or replace your matching experience.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. Which pages generate the most questions before a person reaches provider search or booking?
2. Do you know why someone who sees no immediate opening does not convert to a callback or alternative path?
3. How does a digital visitor with mixed service, coverage and availability questions receive help without calling?
4. Is post-click conversion across therapy and psychiatry paths a current product or marketing priority?

### Close

> If that is a real issue, could we schedule 20 minutes with the digital-acquisition and patient-access owners? We can map one narrow journey and decide whether a pilot has a measurable case.

### Objection handling

**“We already have provider search and booking.”**

> That is the destination, not the thing I am trying to replace. Tars helps a visitor who is not yet ready to self-filter get to the right existing destination.

**“We do not want AI giving mental-health advice.”**

> Agreed. The scope is deliberately non-clinical: service, coverage, process, location and scheduling information, with explicit escalation to your team and crisis resources.

## Michael Midgette — Chief Growth Officer

### Opening

> Hi Michael, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know I caught you out of the blue—may I have 30 seconds to explain why I called, and you can tell me whether it is relevant?

### Reason for the call

> I was looking at Thriveworks’ scale and access promise: 2,600-plus clinicians, more than 340 locations, broad insurance coverage and care as soon as the same week. The growth question is whether every high-intent visitor can reach the right existing next step when they are unsure about service, location, coverage or availability.

### Value proposition

> Tars can run a governed, non-clinical first conversation using Thriveworks-approved information. It captures what the visitor needs and routes them to provider search, booking, callback or scheduling support with a summary. The goal is not a chatbot metric; it is a better qualified handoff and fewer lost high-intent visits.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. Where do prospective clients most commonly fall out between initial interest and a bookable path?
2. How do you handle visitors for whom the first provider search does not show a suitable opening?
3. Does the scheduling team receive enough context from digital inquiries to help without repeating discovery?
4. Which service lines or markets would make a clean acquisition-path pilot meaningful?

### Close

> If one of those is material, would it be unreasonable to schedule 20 minutes with the growth and access-operations leaders? We can build a simple business case around one journey rather than propose a broad platform change.

### Objection handling

**“This is more of an operations issue.”**

> Operations needs to be involved. It is also a growth issue because the experiment begins with a high-intent visit and ends when a person reaches the correct existing access workflow.

**“We already offer phone support.”**

> That is important. Tars is designed to complement it by answering approved questions immediately and giving the support team the visitor’s context when a human conversation is needed.
