# Prosperity Behavioral Health — Cold-Call Scripts

## James Furbush — Vice President of Marketing

### Opening

> Hi James, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know this is unexpected—can I take 30 seconds to explain why I called, and you can decide whether it is relevant?

### Reason for the call

> I saw your focus on building qualified inbound and making marketing activity actually useful for sales. Prosperity’s buyers are treatment-center operators with complicated questions around operations, revenue cycle, outcomes, systems, and growth. I’m calling about how to capture that context before it turns into a generic form fill.

### Value proposition

> Tars gives B2B teams a governed AI conversation on high-intent pages. It uses Prosperity-approved content to answer initial questions, captures organization profile, pain point, urgency, and contact details, then writes a structured summary into HubSpot or the existing sales workflow. It is built to give sales a better starting point, not generate more unqualified chat.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. Which inbound pages generate the most interest but the least sales-ready context?
2. What does sales still have to discover after an inbound form is submitted?
3. How do you distinguish content interest from an active treatment-organization buying initiative?
4. Is handoff quality from marketing to sales a priority this quarter?

### Close

> If that is material, could we schedule 20 minutes with marketing and revenue? We can map one narrow journey and decide whether a controlled pilot has a measurable case.

### Objection handling

**“We already use HubSpot.”**

> Great—Tars complements it. It can collect richer intent and qualification data, then write the summary and fields into your existing HubSpot workflow.

**“We need human discovery for a complex sale.”**

> Absolutely. This does not replace discovery. It gives the human seller a more useful first brief so they can spend time on the real issue instead of basic orientation.

## Chris Powell — Chief Executive Officer

### Opening

> Hi Chris, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know I caught you out of the blue—may I have 30 seconds to explain why I called, and you can decide whether it is relevant?

### Reason for the call

> Prosperity helps treatment organizations navigate issues where operations, technology, reimbursement, growth, and outcomes overlap. I wondered how a prospective client with that kind of complex problem gets from a first web visit to the right human advisory conversation without reducing it to a generic contact form.

### Value proposition

> Tars can create a governed commercial front door using Prosperity-approved content. It clarifies the visitor’s organization, challenge, and timeframe, answers basic fit questions, and routes a structured handoff to the existing team. Prosperity’s advisors retain all solution design and performance recommendations.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. Where do prospective clients lose context between their first website visit and an advisory call?
2. What information would make the first sales conversation more productive?
3. Are there service lines where buyers routinely need basic orientation before engaging?
4. Is improving the commercial experience part of your growth plan?

### Close

> If one pathway is worth improving, would it be unreasonable to schedule 20 minutes with the commercial and operating owners? We can assess a single use case without proposing a broad platform change.

### Objection handling

**“Our work is too customized for an agent.”**

> The advisory work is customized. The initial conversation can be deliberately narrow: approved service information and the basic context your team needs to start a productive human discussion.

**“We do not want AI making claims about outcomes.”**

> Agreed. The agent would use only approved content, avoid outcome promises, and route complex questions to your team.
