# Rula Health — Cold-Call Scripts

## Andrew Mok — Chief Marketing Officer

### Opening

> Hi Andrew, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know this is unexpected — 30 seconds to explain why I called, then you decide if it's relevant?

### Reason for the call

> I've been looking at Rula's growth — provider network roughly doubling in about a year, plus a 48-hour speed-to-care promise. Given your background scaling Turo's marketplace, I'd guess you think a lot about demand-to-supply matching. I wanted to ask whether intake — the step that feeds your matching system — has kept pace with how fast the provider side has grown.

### Value proposition

> Tars gives behavioral-health platforms a HIPAA-ready agent for that non-clinical intake conversation — gathering insurance plan, care type, and format preference conversationally, then handing off cleanly to Rula's existing matching system. It doesn't touch the matching logic or clinical decisions.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. How do you currently measure whether a visitor's first match is actually the right one?
2. Has intake kept the same shape as the provider network has scaled, or has it evolved with it?
3. Is protecting the 48-hour promise something marketing owns, or is that more of a shared metric?
4. Would a single acquisition channel be a clean way to test a change here?

### Close

> If that's worth exploring, could we get 20 minutes with the right growth and product counterparts? I'd scope it to one channel first rather than propose anything broad.

### Objection handling

**"Our matching system already works well."**

> Agreed — I'm not proposing changes there. This is about what feeds it: how completely intake captures plan, care type, and urgency before matching even starts.

**"We're focused on network growth right now, not funnel work."**

> That's exactly why intake matters more, not less — a bigger network only helps if visitors get matched to the right part of it the first time.

## Laura Palmer — Chief Revenue Officer

### Opening

> Hi Laura, this is [Your Name] with Tars — I know this is out of the blue. 30 seconds to explain why I called?

### Reason for the call

> I was looking at Rula's growth story — a provider network that's roughly doubled in a year, and a strong 48-hour speed-to-care promise. Given your background scaling revenue at Google Cloud and Unity, I wanted to ask about the step just before conversion: how much visibility revenue ops has into intake-to-match completion specifically.

### Value proposition

> Tars runs a governed, non-clinical intake conversation — collecting plan, care type, and preferences — then hands off into Rula's existing matching system. The goal isn't a chatbot metric, it's more completed matches from demand you're already generating.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. Do you track completed-intake-to-match rate as its own metric, separate from top-of-funnel traffic?
2. Where does revenue ops currently see the sharpest drop-off in the acquisition funnel?
3. Is protecting the 48-hour promise something you're actively instrumenting for?
4. Would one market or channel be a reasonable scope for a first test?

### Close

> Would 15–20 minutes be worth it to map this against one channel and see if the numbers justify a pilot?

### Objection handling

**"This sounds like a marketing initiative, not a revenue one."**

> It touches both — but since every completed intake is a revenue event, I wanted revenue ops to have visibility before this became a marketing-only conversation.

**"We're mid-scale on the provider side right now — bad timing."**

> Understandable — though that's arguably the best time to fix intake, since a growing network only pays off if visitors get matched correctly against it.

### If voicemail

> Hi Laura, [Your Name] from Tars — we help behavioral-health platforms convert more completed intakes by turning the intake form into a conversation. I'll follow up by email — talk soon.
