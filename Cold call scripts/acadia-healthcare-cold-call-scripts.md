# Acadia Healthcare — Cold-Call Scripts

## Debbie K. Osteen — Chief Executive Officer

### Opening

> Hi Debbie, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know this is unexpected—can I take 30 seconds to explain why I called, and you can decide whether it is relevant?

### Reason for the call

> Acadia has a remarkable breadth of behavioral-health settings, programs, populations, and locations. That is a strength, but it can make the first online step difficult for someone who knows they need help but is not sure which existing Acadia path applies. That is the access moment I’m calling about.

### Value proposition

> Tars gives behavioral-health teams a governed, non-clinical AI conversation. It uses Acadia-approved information to answer process questions, gather a few routing details, and direct the visitor to the existing finder, helpline, placement service, or human admissions path with their context intact. It does not assess, diagnose, decide level of care, or replace clinical judgment.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. Which online journeys create the most uncertainty before a person reaches an admissions or placement team?
2. How does a visitor with questions spanning location, population, program, and coverage get help today?
3. Where do people repeat basic information after moving from the website to a human team?
4. Is access consistency across the network a current patient-experience priority?

### Close

> If that is material, could we schedule 20 minutes with patient access and admissions leaders? We can map one narrow journey and decide whether a controlled pilot has a measurable case.

### Objection handling

**“We cannot allow AI to guide treatment.”**

> Agreed. The scope is deliberately non-clinical: approved logistics and routing only, with immediate human and emergency escalation. Clinical and admissions decisions stay with Acadia teams.

**“We already have a helpline and facility finder.”**

> Those remain the destinations. Tars helps the visitor who is not yet confident which destination is appropriate get there with more context.

## David Duckworth — Interim Chief Financial Officer

### Opening

> Hi David, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know I caught you out of the blue—may I have 30 seconds to explain why I called, and you can decide whether it is relevant?

### Reason for the call

> At Acadia’s scale, a visitor may arrive with a practical question about location, program, population, or insurance before they reach one of your established access routes. I’m calling about whether one governed digital path could be tested to make those existing handoffs more complete and measurable.

### Value proposition

> Tars can run a narrow, non-clinical conversation using only Acadia-approved content. It captures minimal routing information and sends the visitor to the current finder, helpline, placement, or admissions workflow with a summary. The pilot is measured on appropriate next-step completion and handoff quality—not clinical outcomes or generic chat volume.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. Is visitor-to-admissions/placement handoff quality measured today?
2. Which digital pages create the most incomplete or unstructured inquiries?
3. What fields would help receiving teams avoid repeating basic discovery?
4. Would one page or market be a suitable controlled test?

### Close

> If there is a measurable path, would it be unreasonable to schedule 20 minutes with access, finance, and operations stakeholders? We can assess one use case without proposing a broad platform change.

### Objection handling

**“We need evidence before adding technology.”**

> That is exactly the approach: one path, a defined baseline, controlled routing, and agreed measures before any broader decision.

**“This belongs with operations.”**

> Operations and admissions should co-own the design. Finance matters because the pilot must be scoped with a clear measure and expand only if the existing process demonstrably improves.
