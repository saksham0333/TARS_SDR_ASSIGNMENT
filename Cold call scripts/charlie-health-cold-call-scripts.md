# Charlie Health — Cold-Call Scripts

## Carter Barnhart — Co-founder & CEO

### Opening

> Hi Carter, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know this is an unexpected call—can I take 30 seconds to explain why I called, and you can decide whether it's relevant?

### Reason for the call

> I've been following Charlie Health's growth to 39-plus states and 600-plus insurance plans, and the mission behind it—you've spoken about how capacity constraints and insurance barriers kept people from getting crisis-level care in time. As that reach grows, I imagine the first few minutes of someone reaching out—often a family in crisis—matters more than ever. Given you're the founder closest to that mission, I thought it might be relevant.

### Value proposition

> Tars gives behavioral-health teams a HIPAA-ready agent for the non-clinical first conversation. It uses Charlie Health-approved content to explain what IOP is, check insurance and Medicaid eligibility, and capture urgency and referral context—then hands the family straight to your Admissions Team with a summary attached. It never makes a clinical or risk decision; that stays fully with your team and crisis protocols.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. When a family reaches out in crisis, how much do they already understand about IOP versus inpatient or weekly therapy before Admissions gets on the phone?
2. How does insurance/Medicaid eligibility get communicated before that first Admissions call today?
3. Is reducing time-to-verified-intake, especially for high-urgency inquiries, a current priority?
4. Do referral partners (providers, schools, discharge planners) and direct families come in through the same intake path, or different ones?

### Close

> If any of that is a real gap, could we schedule 20 minutes with you and whoever owns Admissions or growth operations? We'd map one narrow journey—say, first-contact triage—and see if a pilot has a measurable case.

### Objection handling

**"Our Admissions Team already verifies insurance and handles intake."**

> That should stay exactly as it is—Tars sits before that call, making sure Admissions gets a family that's already been asked the right questions, with urgency and context already captured.

**"We can't have AI making decisions with a high-acuity, crisis-adjacent population."**

> Agreed, and it wouldn't. The scope is deliberately non-clinical: what IOP is, insurance and process. Any sign of active crisis triggers immediate escalation to your team and crisis resources—never left to the agent.

## Noah Weissmann — VP, Growth Operations

### Opening

> Hi Noah, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know I caught you out of the blue—may I have 30 seconds to explain why I called, and you can tell me whether it's relevant?

### Reason for the call

> I was looking at Charlie Health's scale—39-plus states, 600-plus insurance plans, and a 24-hour start-of-care goal. That's an operationally tight model. The growth question is whether every inbound inquiry—direct or referral—gets pre-qualified consistently before it reaches Admissions, especially given how capacity-sensitive this business is.

### Value proposition

> Tars can run a governed, non-clinical first conversation using Charlie Health-approved information. It captures state, insurance status, referral source and urgency, then routes the inquiry into your existing Admissions or intake workflow with a summary. The goal isn't a chatbot metric—it's better-qualified handoffs and cleaner capacity planning on your side.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. How much variation is there today in how well-qualified an inquiry is by the time it reaches Admissions?
2. Do referral-driven and direct-to-consumer inquiries get routed and triaged differently?
3. Where do you see capacity or staffing strain tied to unqualified or misrouted inbound volume?
4. Would a pilot on one channel—say your direct-to-consumer intake form—be a clean way to test this?

### Close

> If one of those is material, would it be unreasonable to set up 20 minutes with you and whoever owns Admissions? We can build a simple business case around one channel rather than propose a broad platform change.

### Objection handling

**"We already have a fast, 24-hour intake process."**

> That speed is the reason this fits well—Tars would sit at the very front of that funnel, so the information Admissions needs is already captured before the clock even starts.

**"We're cautious about anything touching insurance or clinical eligibility, especially for this population."**

> Understood—the agent doesn't determine eligibility or make clinical calls. It captures what the family believes about their coverage and urgency; your Admissions and clinical teams still do all verification and intake decisions.
