# Lightfully Behavioral Health — Cold-Call Scripts

## Britt Freiden — Chief Strategy Officer

### Opening

> Hi Britt, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know this is unexpected—can I take 30 seconds to explain why I called, and you can decide whether it is relevant?

### Reason for the call

> I was reviewing Lightfully’s admissions journey. It is thoughtfully human—counselor connection, insurance verification, clinical assessment, then admission planning. The question I’m calling about is the visitor who has a practical question about program, location, coverage, or what happens next before they are ready to call.

### Value proposition

> Tars provides a governed, non-clinical AI conversation using Lightfully-approved content. It answers operational questions, collects a few routing details, and passes the visitor to the existing Admissions Concierge Team with context preserved. It does not diagnose, assess clinical fit, or recommend treatment.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. Where do visitors most often pause before connecting with admissions?
2. Which pre-admissions questions create the most volume for counselors?
3. Do counselors receive enough context from digital inquiries to avoid repeating basics?
4. Is a consistent access layer part of the systems strategy for new markets or programs?

### Close

> If that is material, could we schedule 20 minutes with strategy, admissions, and digital? We can map one narrow flow and decide whether it has a measurable case.

### Objection handling

**“We already have admissions counselors.”**

> They remain essential. Tars helps the visitor before the call and gives the counselor a better-prepared handoff; it does not replace the counselor or clinical assessment.

**“We do not want AI involved in care decisions.”**

> Agreed. The scope is deliberately non-clinical, with approved operational content and immediate human/crisis escalation.

## Jennifer Steiner — Chief Executive Officer

### Opening

> Hi Jennifer, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know I caught you out of the blue—may I have 30 seconds to explain why I reached out, and you can decide whether it is relevant?

### Reason for the call

> Lightfully has built a compassionate, counselor-led admissions process. I wondered how a visitor who is not yet ready to call—but has a practical question about the process, location, or insurance—gets immediate help without losing their momentum.

### Value proposition

> Tars can create a safe, non-clinical digital front door using Lightfully-approved information. It provides approved operational answers, gathers minimal routing context, and transfers that summary to the existing admissions workflow. Lightfully’s clinicians and counselors retain all care and admission decisions.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. How do you currently support visitors with questions before they are ready to reach admissions?
2. Where do people have to repeat information after submitting a message or calling?
3. What guardrails would be non-negotiable for a conversational experience?
4. Is improving the first digital moment part of Lightfully’s patient-experience priorities?

### Close

> If one pathway is worth improving, would it be unreasonable to schedule 20 minutes with the patient-experience and admissions leaders? We can assess one use case without proposing a broad platform change.

### Objection handling

**“Mental health conversations are too sensitive for AI.”**

> They are sensitive, which is why the scope is narrow: no therapy, diagnosis, or clinical decision. It handles only approved operational information and knows when to escalate to a human or emergency resource.

**“Our existing process is intentionally human.”**

> That is exactly what the design protects. The agent supports the period before a person reaches your team, then hands off rather than taking over the relationship.
