# Grow Therapy — Cold-Call Scripts

## Marshall Ball — SVP, Marketing

### Opening

> Hi Marshall, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know this is out of nowhere — 30 seconds to explain why I called, and you tell me if it's relevant?

### Reason for the call

> I've been looking at Grow's ambition to build a widely recognized, beloved brand in this category — and at the search experience that follows the brand campaign, across 90+ specialties and 25,000+ providers. Given your background at The Farmer's Dog and Expedia, I'd guess brand-to-product consistency is something you think about a lot. I wanted to ask how that search moment holds up against the brand promise today.

### Value proposition

> Tars gives behavioral-health marketplaces a HIPAA-ready agent for that non-clinical first conversation — translating a visitor's plain-language answers into the right filters, so the search experience feels as considered as the brand campaign that brought them there.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. Do you have visibility into where visitors drop off between arriving and completing a search?
2. Is the new employer/EAP-transition channel something marketing owns end-to-end, or shared with growth/product?
3. How do you currently think about brand consistency carrying through into the actual product experience?
4. Would the new employer channel be a reasonable place to test something new, given it has no legacy page?

### Close

> If that's worth exploring, could we get 20 minutes with the right growth and product counterparts? I'd scope this to the new employer channel first.

### Objection handling

**"Our search and filters already work well."**

> They're clearly built with real depth. The question is whether a first-time visitor knows how to use that depth — that's a separate, testable question from whether the filters themselves are good.

**"We're focused on brand campaigns right now, not funnel mechanics."**

> That's exactly the moment this matters most — a strong campaign that ends at an unfamiliar filter panel loses some of what the campaign built.

## Jason Oringher — Director, Performance Marketing

### Opening

> Hi Jason, this is [Your Name] with Tars — cold call, I know. 30 seconds to explain why I called?

### Reason for the call

> I've been looking at Grow's search experience — strong specialty depth, 25,000+ providers — and thinking about how that holds up for a paid-traffic visitor who's already signaled intent by clicking an ad. Given your performance-marketing background, I wanted to ask where you currently see the sharpest drop-off between click and a completed search.

### Value proposition

> Tars replaces the initial search step with a conversational flow — translating a few plain-language answers into the right filters — integrated with your existing paid tracking, so any lift shows up in your own attribution.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. Do you track completed-search-to-booking rate by channel today?
2. Is the new employer/EAP-transition channel already being measured separately from the rest of the funnel?
3. Where do you see the most paid spend "leaking" in the current funnel?
4. Would a single channel — like the new employer channel — be a reasonable first test?

### Close

> Would 15 minutes be worth it to map this against one channel, probably the new employer one, and see if the numbers justify a pilot?

### Objection handling

**"Our filters are already really strong."**

> Agreed — the depth is real. This isn't about replacing the filters, it's about how a visitor gets into them in the first place.

**"Sounds like a chatbot — we've seen those pitched before."**

> Fair — most of those are support widgets. This replaces the search entry point itself and is measured on conversion, not engagement.

### If voicemail

> Hi Jason, [Your Name] from Tars — we help healthcare marketplaces convert more paid traffic by turning the search step into a conversation. I'll follow up by email — talk soon.
