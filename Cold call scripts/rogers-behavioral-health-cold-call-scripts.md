# Rogers Behavioral Health — Cold-Call Scripts

## Cindy Meyer — President & Chief Executive Officer

### Opening

> Hi Cindy, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know this is unexpected—can I take 30 seconds to explain why I called, and you can decide whether it is relevant?

### Reason for the call

> Rogers has a broad, specialized network across conditions, age groups, program levels, and states. That is an advantage once someone knows where to go. The harder case is a person or family who has a question spanning program, location, age, or coverage before they know which existing Rogers admissions route fits. That is the access moment I’m calling about.

### Value proposition

> Tars provides a governed, non-clinical AI conversation using Rogers-approved content. It can answer process questions, collect a few routing details, and direct the person to the existing admissions, care-coordination, or location workflow with a concise summary. It does not diagnose, recommend treatment, decide eligibility, or determine level of care.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. Which online journeys create the most uncertainty before someone reaches admissions?
2. How does a visitor who is unsure of the best Rogers location get help today?
3. Where do people repeat basic information after moving from the website to a human team?
4. Is access consistency across the expanding network a current priority?

### Close

> If that is material, could we schedule 20 minutes with admissions and care-coordination leaders? We can map one narrow journey and decide whether a controlled pilot has a measurable case.

### Objection handling

**“We cannot allow AI to guide treatment.”**

> Agreed. The scope is intentionally non-clinical: approved logistics and routing only, with clear human and emergency escalation. Clinical decisions stay with Rogers teams.

**“We already have admissions counselors.”**

> They remain the destination. Tars helps the visitor before the call and gives the counselor basic context so the human conversation can start further ahead.

## Jeanette Osborn — Vice President, Care Coordination

### Opening

> Hi Jeanette, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know I caught you out of the blue—may I have 30 seconds to explain why I called, and you can decide whether it is relevant?

### Reason for the call

> I was looking at Rogers’ online entry points: program and condition information, location guidance, admissions screening, and online outreach. I wondered whether the person’s basic non-clinical context is always retained as they move from that first digital interaction to the right human team.

### Value proposition

> Tars can run a narrow, governed conversation using only Rogers-approved content. It answers operational questions, captures location, age pathway, broad interest, coverage and contact preference, then directs the visitor to the existing workflow with a summary. It does not create a clinical intake process or replace your team’s judgment.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. Where are visitors most likely to repeat information after an online inquiry?
2. What context would help admissions or care coordination start the first conversation more effectively?
3. Which questions are safe and useful to answer before a person reaches a human?
4. Is there one condition or location path that would be appropriate for a controlled test?

### Close

> If one pathway is worth improving, would it be unreasonable to schedule 20 minutes with care coordination and admissions? We can assess a single use case without proposing a broad platform change.

### Objection handling

**“These conversations are too sensitive for AI.”**

> They are sensitive, which is why the design is limited to approved operational information, immediate escalation, and fast handoff—not clinical guidance or counseling.

**“We do not want another workflow.”**

> The goal is to feed the existing workflow, not create another one. The agent routes to your current team and passes a concise context summary.
