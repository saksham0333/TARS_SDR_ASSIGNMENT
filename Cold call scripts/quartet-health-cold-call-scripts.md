# Quartet Health — Cold-Call Scripts

## Karen Mitchell — Chief Growth Officer

### Opening

> Hi Karen, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know this is unexpected—can I take 30 seconds to explain why I called, and you can decide whether it is relevant?

### Reason for the call

> I was looking at Quartet’s enterprise story for health plans, health systems, and community partners. A visitor may understand that behavioral-health access is a priority but still have questions about footprint, care model, partner fit, or deployment before they are ready to complete a contact form. That is the commercial moment I’m calling about.

### Value proposition

> Tars provides a governed AI conversation for enterprise acquisition pages. It uses Quartet-approved content to answer initial questions, captures organization type, use case, market, timeline and contact details, and routes a structured summary to the right commercial owner. It is not a replacement for Quartet’s Care Navigation, matching, or patient-care model.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. Where do partner prospects most often stall before requesting a meeting?
2. Which questions do sales teams repeatedly answer on the first call?
3. How do you distinguish a health-plan, system, or community-partner inquiry before seller follow-up?
4. Is web-to-qualified-opportunity conversion a current demand-generation priority?

### Close

> If that is material, could we schedule 20 minutes with growth and demand generation? We can map one narrow partner journey and decide whether a controlled pilot has a measurable case.

### Objection handling

**“We already have a contact form.”**

> The form remains useful. Tars adds the short conversation before it, so the buyer has an approved answer and the seller receives qualification context rather than a blank request.

**“We already have sophisticated patient-matching technology.”**

> Exactly—and I am not proposing to replace it. This is an enterprise acquisition use case, separate from care navigation and clinical workflows.

## Christopher Molaro — CEO & Founder, NeuroFlow

### Opening

> Hi Christopher, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know I caught you out of the blue—may I have 30 seconds to explain why I called, and you can decide whether it is relevant?

### Reason for the call

> With Quartet now part of NeuroFlow, enterprise buyers have a broader but more complex set of behavioral-health capabilities to understand. I wondered how a buyer with a cross-portfolio question gets to the right commercial conversation without first having to untangle brands, products, and teams.

### Value proposition

> Tars can provide a single, governed acquisition conversation using approved content from the combined portfolio. It identifies the buyer and use case, answers first-party questions, and routes a structured handoff to the right existing team. It stays deliberately outside patient matching, care navigation, and clinical decisions.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. Is a unified enterprise website journey part of the post-acquisition agenda?
2. Where do inbound leads lose context when moving between NeuroFlow and Quartet pathways?
3. Which buyer questions most often require a seller to provide basic orientation?
4. What would a better-qualified first commercial meeting be worth to the growth team?

### Close

> If one path is worth improving, would it be unreasonable to schedule 20 minutes with the commercial and digital owners? We can evaluate a single use case without proposing a broad platform change.

### Objection handling

**“Integration work is already complex.”**

> That is why I’d start with one high-intent page and route into your current processes. The test can be small, governed, and additive.

**“We do not want an AI agent answering clinical questions.”**

> Agreed. The proposed use case is enterprise-facing. Any patient-facing experience would be limited to approved logistics and immediate routing to Quartet’s existing care journey or emergency resources.
