# Discovery Behavioral Health — Cold-Call Scripts

## Timothy Johnson — Chief Growth Officer

### Opening

> Hi Timothy, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know this is unexpected—can I take 30 seconds to explain why I called, and you can decide whether it is relevant?

### Reason for the call

> I was reviewing DBH’s nationwide continuum: 130-plus programs across multiple brands, needs, locations, and levels of care. That is powerful once a person knows the right program. The harder case is a family with a combination of condition, age, coverage, location, and timing questions before they know which DBH path to take.

### Value proposition

> Tars gives behavioral-health teams a governed, non-clinical AI conversation. It uses only DBH-approved content, captures routing context, and hands the person to the correct existing brand or admissions workflow with a summary. It does not diagnose, recommend treatment, or decide eligibility.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. Where do prospective patients fall out before reaching an admissions conversation?
2. How does someone who is unsure which DBH program fits get help online today?
3. Do admissions teams have enough context from web inquiries to avoid starting from zero?
4. Which service line or market would be meaningful for a measurable pilot?

### Close

> If that is material, could we schedule 20 minutes with growth, admissions, and digital? We can map one narrow path and decide whether a controlled test has a business case.

### Objection handling

**“We already have a location finder.”**

> That remains the destination. Tars helps the person who cannot yet self-select a state, brand, or program get to the right existing destination.

**“We cannot use AI for behavioral-health advice.”**

> Agreed. The scope is deliberately non-clinical: approved program, location, coverage, and admissions-process information, with defined human and emergency escalation.

## Pete Clarke — Chief Executive Officer

### Opening

> Hi Pete, this is [Your Name] with Tars. Congratulations on the new role. I know this is an unexpected call—may I have 30 seconds to explain why I reached out, and you can decide whether it is relevant?

### Reason for the call

> DBH has a differentiated national portfolio and is entering a new chapter with HPS. I wondered how consistently a patient or family who is unsure which program fits gets from a first online question to the appropriate existing admissions team—without navigating the full portfolio alone.

### Value proposition

> Tars can provide a consistent, governed, non-clinical first conversation across DBH’s digital entry points. DBH sets enterprise safety, privacy and content rules; individual brands keep their admissions, availability and human-handoff controls. The goal is a more informed existing handoff, not automation of clinical decisions.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. Is pre-admissions access consistency across brands part of the current operating agenda?
2. Where does a visitor lose context when moving from a corporate page to a brand or admissions team?
3. Which guardrails would be non-negotiable for a digital conversation in DBH’s environment?
4. Where could a narrowly scoped test reduce friction without changing the clinical model?

### Close

> If one pathway is worth improving, would it be unreasonable to schedule 20 minutes with the access and operating owners? We can evaluate a single use case rather than propose a broad platform change.

### Objection handling

**“Our brands and programs are different.”**

> That is why the model is central guardrails with brand-level content, routing, and escalation controls—not one generic script.

**“This belongs with admissions.”**

> Admissions should co-own the design. It is also an enterprise experience issue because the interaction starts before a person knows which admission team to contact.
