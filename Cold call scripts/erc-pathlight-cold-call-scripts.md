# Eating Recovery Center & Pathlight — Cold-Call Script

## Goran Dragolovic — Chief Executive Officer

### Opening

> Hi Goran, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know this is unexpected—can I take 30 seconds to explain why I called, and you can decide whether it is relevant?

### Reason for the call

> I was reviewing ERC Pathlight’s public access experience. You offer several ways to connect, a clinically led assessment, insurance support, and a continuum of in-person and virtual care. That is a strong foundation. The harder case is a patient, caregiver, or referrer with several questions—program fit, age, location, coverage, and next steps—before they are ready to reach the admissions team. Given your focus on growth and patient outcomes, I thought it might be relevant.

### Value proposition

> Tars helps healthcare teams run a governed, non-clinical first conversation using approved information. It can answer operational questions, capture limited routing context, and move the person to ERC Pathlight’s existing assessment, admissions, insurance, or referral workflow with a short summary. It does not diagnose, perform a clinical assessment, or make a level-of-care decision; the ERC Pathlight team retains that responsibility.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. Where do patients, caregivers, or referral sources most often get stuck before they reach the assessment team?
2. Do your admissions specialists have to repeat non-clinical basics a digital journey could capture safely?
3. How do you measure whether a high-intent visitor reaches the appropriate human next step?
4. Is there a program, market, or referral pathway where a clearer first conversation would matter most?

### Close

> If that friction is real, would it be unreasonable to schedule 20 minutes with the patient-access, clinical, and compliance owners? We can map one narrow journey and decide whether a small pilot has a measurable case.

### Objection handling

**“We already have admissions and a clinical assessment.”**

> That is the destination I would preserve. Tars would not replace it; it helps someone who is still uncertain reach the correct existing human path with useful, non-clinical context.

**“We cannot have AI giving mental-health advice.”**

> Agreed. The scope is deliberately non-clinical: approved information about programs, access, coverage, and process, with clear escalation to humans and emergency resources. No diagnosis, risk assessment, or care recommendation.

**“This is an admissions-operations initiative.”**

> Admissions, clinical, and compliance should all be involved. It is also a growth and experience question because it begins with high-intent demand and succeeds only when a person reaches the correct existing next step.
