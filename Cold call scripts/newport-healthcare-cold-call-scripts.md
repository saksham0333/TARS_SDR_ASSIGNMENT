# Newport Healthcare — Cold-Call Script

## Kristin Petrone Wilson — Chief Growth & Experience Officer

### Opening

> Hi Kristin, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know this is unexpected—can I take 30 seconds to explain why I called, and you can decide whether it is relevant?

### Reason for the call

> I was reviewing Newport’s public family-access experience. You have 24/7 admissions support, a full continuum of care, insurance guidance, and a contact path that gathers age, location, and coverage. That is a strong foundation. The harder case is a family with several questions—whether Newport serves their child’s age, what program may be available nearby, how insurance works, and what they should do next—before they are ready to call. Given your growth-and-experience remit, I thought it might be relevant.

### Value proposition

> Tars helps healthcare teams run a governed, non-clinical first conversation using their approved information. It can answer operational questions, collect limited routing context, and move the visitor into the existing Newport admissions or referral workflow with a short summary. It does not diagnose, provide clinical advice, or make a level-of-care decision; Newport’s clinicians and admissions team remain in control.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. Where do families or referral sources most often get stuck before they reach admissions?
2. Do admissions specialists have to repeat basic non-clinical discovery that a digital journey could capture safely?
3. How do you measure whether a family who begins online reaches the appropriate human next step?
4. Is there a particular program, region, or high-intent page where a clearer first conversation would matter most?

### Close

> If that friction is real, would it be unreasonable to schedule 20 minutes with the growth, admissions, and compliance owners? We can map one narrow journey and determine whether a small pilot has a measurable case.

### Objection handling

**“We already have a 24/7 admissions line and form.”**

> That is exactly the destination I would preserve. Tars would not replace it; it helps visitors who are still uncertain get to the right existing human path with useful context.

**“We cannot have AI giving mental-health advice.”**

> Agreed. The scope should be deliberately non-clinical: approved information about programs, eligibility, locations, insurance process, and next steps—with clear immediate escalation to humans and emergency resources. No diagnosis, treatment advice, or care decision.

**“This sounds like an admissions-operations project.”**

> Admissions and compliance should be involved. It is also an experience and growth question because the interaction starts with high-intent demand and succeeds only when the family reaches the correct existing next step.
