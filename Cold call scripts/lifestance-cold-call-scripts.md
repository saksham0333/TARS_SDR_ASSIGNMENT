# LifeStance Health — Cold-Call Scripts

## Dan Ferris — Chief Growth Officer

### Opening

> Hi Dan, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know this is unexpected—can I take 30 seconds to explain why I called, and you can decide whether it is relevant?

### Reason for the call

> I was looking at LifeStance’s public patient journey. A new visitor chooses therapy, medication or testing, then needs to reconcile location, insurance and in-person versus telehealth before entering provider matching and booking. Given that you lead growth across marketing, clinician recruiting and business development, I wondered whether that first digital handoff is a priority.

### Value proposition

> Tars helps behavioral-health teams with the *non-clinical* first conversation. The agent uses approved LifeStance content to answer operational questions, captures the context needed for routing and sends the visitor to the existing search, booking or access workflow with a summary. It does not make clinical decisions or replace the care team.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. Where do prospective patients most often stall before a successful provider match or appointment?
2. How are after-hours questions about insurance, service type or visit format handled today?
3. Does the scheduling/access team need to re-collect information a visitor has already provided online?
4. Which service lines or markets have enough high-intent traffic to make an acquisition-path experiment meaningful?

### Close

> If one of those is a material issue, would it be unreasonable to schedule 20 minutes with the person who owns patient access and digital acquisition? We can map one narrow journey and determine whether a pilot is justified.

### Objection handling

**“We already have digital matching.”**

> That is exactly where Tars fits. I am not proposing a replacement. The agent serves the visitor who has not yet reached the matching flow because they are still resolving a practical question in plain language.

**“AI is risky in behavioral health.”**

> I agree where clinical assessment is involved. The scope here is explicitly non-clinical: approved program, insurance, location and process information, plus defined escalation to LifeStance people and crisis resources.

## Ashley Anderson — SVP, Marketing

### Opening

> Hi Ashley, this is [Your Name] with Tars. I know I caught you out of the blue—may I take 30 seconds to explain why I called, and you can tell me whether it is worth a follow-up?

### Reason for the call

> I noticed that LifeStance’s acquisition journey already makes strong promises around choice of care, insurance coverage and affordability. But a visitor still has to answer the question, “What does this mean for my state, plan and preferred visit type?” before booking. I called because that is often a post-click conversion problem, not just a contact-center problem.

### Value proposition

> Tars gives marketing teams a governed conversational layer that makes those approved claims interactive. It can answer non-clinical questions, collect routing details and move the visitor into the existing LifeStance provider-search, booking or human-support path—while maintaining brand voice and measurement.

### Discovery questions

Use two:

1. Which LifeStance landing pages create the most high-intent questions before a visitor gets to booking?
2. Do you currently know why visitors leave a therapy, psychiatry or insurance page without entering the matching flow?
3. How do campaign visitors with insurance or telehealth questions receive an answer when they are not ready to call?
4. Is there an active 2026 initiative to improve post-click conversion or patient-access journeys?

### Close

> If there is a relevant page or campaign, could we schedule 20 minutes to show a deliberately narrow test: approved Q&A, a few routing fields and a handoff into your existing flow? We would measure whether it earns its place.

### Objection handling

**“We do not want a generic chatbot.”**

> Neither do we. The agent is built around the exact decisions LifeStance already asks a visitor to make, with clear limits on what it can say and a defined next step for every route.

**“This belongs to operations, not marketing.”**

> Operations should be involved. Marketing still benefits because the experiment starts at the post-click moment, preserves campaign context and can improve the quality of demand that reaches access and scheduling.
